Many of the other 'bacteria' only ASVs are actually likely to be mitochondria or 18S reads that did not get annotated as much using Silva or GG but when I blasted them against the NCBI NR database ... at lot of hits were to phytoplankton and apicomplexans (parasites ...Cephaloidophora cf. communi; that was pretty frequent. maybe should look into this guy a bit more.). 

So it will be safest to remove the following ASVs from the dataset.

Those ASVs that have an 'unknown' or 'bacteria' only annotation as we cannot confirm if they are truly bacteria or unannotated mitochondria or chloroplasts. Also prudent to remove Proteobacteria only and Alphaproteobacteria only ASV because we cannot confirm or deny if they are mitochondria that just are not annotated.

How to do this in R:

smic_fix<- smic %>%
 tax_fix(
  min_length = 4,
  unknowns = c("", "NA","unknown","uncultured","unassigned"),
  sep = " ", anon_unique = TRUE,
  suffix_rank = "classified"
 )


#get rid of uninformative groups
smic0<-smic_fix %>% tax_select(tax_list = "Unassigned Domain", deselect = TRUE) %>% tax_select(tax_list = "Bacteria Domain", deselect = TRUE) #12941 taxa and 2015 samples

#we also want to get rid of Proteobacteria Phylum (class) and Alphaproteobacteria Class (family) as these unknown reads could be mitochondrial. I wonder if we should also get rid of Cyanobacteriia Class as it could be unassigned chloroplasts? Think about this.

smic1<-smic0 %>% tax_select(tax_list = "Proteobacteria Phylum", deselect = TRUE) %>% tax_select(tax_list = "Alphaproteobacteria Class", deselect = TRUE)


I suggest to do this right after making your phyloseq object and before decontamination.
